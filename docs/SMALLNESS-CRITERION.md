# Smallness criterion

`contractionGraphSmallnessData` tests a proper birational contraction

```text
f : X -> Y
```

from its biprojective graph.  Its certificate is valid under the standing MMP
assumptions used by this package: the graph is integral, `X` and `Y` are normal
projective varieties, and the induced function-field extension is separable.
The current implementation checks the expected generic differential rank, but
does not independently prove normality or separability of arbitrary graphs
supplied by a caller.

## Criterion

Let `G` be the homogeneous coordinate ring of the affine cone over the graph,
and let `Omega` be the relative Kähler differential module for the projection
to the target-coordinate block.  The generic rank of `Omega` is one: it is the
source cone's scaling direction.  For a generic-rank-one module on an integral
scheme, failure to be locally generated by one element is cut out by

```text
ann(exteriorPower(2, Omega)).
```

On the locus where `f` is an isomorphism, `Omega` is the rank-one scaling
module, so its second exterior power vanishes.  At the generic point of a
positive-dimensional fibre, separability contributes at least one additional
relative differential direction, so the second exterior power is nonzero.

For a proper birational morphism onto a normal variety, the quasi-finite locus
is the isomorphism locus by Zariski's Main Theorem.  Consequently, after
saturation by the two irrelevant ideals, the support of
`exteriorPower(2, Omega)` is exactly the exceptional locus.  The contraction is
small precisely when this support has codimension at least two in `X`.

Weighted projective coordinates do not alter the rank calculation in
characteristic zero: their finite stabilizers are étale.  In positive
characteristic, inseparable maps or non-reduced stabilizers fall outside the
certificate's stated assumptions.

## Computational form

The implementation performs the following operations:

1. construct the relative Jacobian using the source-coordinate columns;
2. form and prune `Omega`;
3. require `rank Omega == 1`;
4. compute `ann(exteriorPower(2, Omega))`;
5. saturate by the product of the source and target irrelevant ideals;
6. subtract the two cone dimensions and compare the resulting codimension
   with two.

Using the exterior power avoids constructing all maximal presentation minors,
which was the dominant cost of the equivalent first-Fitting-ideal
calculation.

## Independent regressions

The integration tests cover all three geometric outcomes needed by the MMP
driver:

- the blow-up of a line in `P^3`, whose exceptional divisor has codimension
  one and is not small;
- the standard small resolution of a projective ordinary double point, whose
  exceptional curve has codimension two and is small;
- the identity of `P^1`, whose exceptional locus is empty.
