Worked examples

Nine worked examples that these packages compute, each with the Macaulay2 session that computes it.

Every example has two parts. First a short explanation of the mathematics. Then the session that does the computation. The sessions are real: the input files in examples/*.m2 are given to Macaulay2, and the output is saved exactly as Macaulay2 printed it. Each session is folded, with its main results shown on the fold. Click one to read all of it. The algorithms and their proofs are in the paper (arXiv:2603.13703).

In Examples 1 to 6, $X$ is a normal projective threefold and $R$ is its homogeneous coordinate ring. Examples 7 to 9 relax that: some of the variables are given degree zero, their coordinates land in $R_0$, and $X = \Proj R$ is then projective over the affine $\Spec R_0$ rather than over a point. In all of them $K_X$ is the canonical divisor and $H$ is an ample Cartier divisor, ample over the base where there is one. The nef threshold is

$$\lambda = \min\{\,t \ge 0 \;:\; K_X + tH \text{ is nef}\,\}.$$

$X$ has to be normal and log terminal. The package assumes this. It does not check it.

In the paper, $X = \Proj R$ is monograded with $R_0 = k$. A bigraded ring appears only as the graph of a morphism, which is how the Stein factorization is computed. Examples 1 to 6 stay inside that setting. Examples 7 to 9 keep the single grading but drop $R_0 = k$, which is the relative setting; the package also has entry points that take a multigraded variety directly, and those go beyond the paper too, but this page does not use them.

Which examples run the whole minimal model program, and which run one stage of it. This matters because they are given different things. Examples 1, 2, 7, 8 and 9 run the minimal model program (MMP) itself: the input is the homogeneous coordinate ring of $X$ and nothing else, and the driver finds every step itself. The others exercise a single stage, and each stage takes what that stage takes — the nef threshold and the contraction again take a ring (Example 3), the Stein factorization takes a morphism, presented as the parametrized graph the paper writes it with (Examples 4 and 5), and the relative canonical model takes the base of a contraction (Example 6). Example 5 does both, in that order, and says where the join is.

1. Projective space

Here $X = \PP^3$ and $K_X = -4H$, where $H$ is the hyperplane class. The example asks three questions.

Is $K_X$ nef? No. The package tests the divisor $K_X + \tfrac12 H$. It checks the first six multiples of that divisor, and none of them is base point free. Six is enough: the effective base point free theorem says so for this case. So $K_X + \tfrac12 H$ is not nef. If $K_X$ were nef, then $K_X + \tfrac12 H$ would be nef too, because $H$ is ample. So $K_X$ is not nef.

What is the nef threshold? $K_X + tH = (t-4)H$, which is nef exactly when $t \ge 4$. So $\lambda = 4$, and the package finds $4$.

What does $K_X + \lambda H$ contract? $K_X + 4H = 0$. It is base point free, and its linear system sends $X$ to one point. This is a fibre type contraction, and the dimension drops by $3$. The MMP driver stops here, after one step, at a $K$-negative fibration.

2. A quintic threefold

$X$ is a smooth quintic in $\PP^4$. By adjunction $K_X = (5-5)H = 0$.

The package tests the pluricanonical divisors first. The first one is $K_X$ itself, and it is base point free. So $K_X$ is nef and $X$ is already a minimal model. The driver stops after zero steps.

3. The Segre threefold

$X = \PP^1 \times \PP^2$, embedded in $\PP^5$ by the Segre map. So $R$ is the quotient by the $2 \times 2$ minors of a $2 \times 3$ matrix of variables.

Here $K_X = \OO(-2,-3)$ and $H = \OO(1,1)$, so $K_X + tH = \OO(t-2,\,t-3)$. This is nef exactly when $t \ge 3$. The package computes $\lambda = 3$, and then computes the contraction given by

$$K_X + 3H = \OO(1,0).$$

The linear system of $\OO(1,0)$ is the projection $X \to \PP^1$. The package finds a fibre type contraction with target dimension $1$.

This time the target is not a point, so there is a Stein factorization to compute. The bound that certifies it is computed too, not assumed. Here the factorization is trivial, because $\mathrm{pr}_1$ already has connected fibres. Example 4 is the same $X$ with a map whose fibres are not connected.

4. A map with disconnected fibres

Stein factorization writes a map $f : Y \to W$ as $f = g \circ h$, where $h$ has connected fibres and $g$ is finite. In examples 3 and 5 the finite map $g$ turns out to be an isomorphism, so the factorization does nothing. This example is one where it does something.

Take $Y = \PP^1 \times \PP^2$ again, and the map

$$f : Y \to \PP^1, \qquad ([s_0:s_1],\,x) \longmapsto [s_0^2 : s_1^2].$$

Over a point of $\PP^1$ other than $[1:0]$ and $[0:1]$, the square map has two preimages, so the fibre of $f$ is two disjoint copies of $\PP^2$. Over those two points the fibre is one copy. The session checks this first, with no help from the package: it decomposes the fibre over $t_0 = t_1$ and gets two pieces, and the fibre over $t_1 = 0$ and gets one.

So $f$ does not have connected fibres, and the factorization has to be

$$Y \;\xrightarrow{\ h = \mathrm{pr}_1\ }\; \PP^1 \;\xrightarrow{\ g = \text{squaring}\ }\; \PP^1 .$$

The package finds exactly this. The middle variety $Z$ has dimension $1$, and $Y$ has dimension $3$, so $h$ has relative dimension $2$. The target also has dimension $1$, so $g$ is finite. And the Stein algebra has rank $2$ over the coordinate ring of the target, which is the degree of $g$. Degree $2$ means $g$ is not an isomorphism.

The same rank is $1$ in example 5, where $g$ is an isomorphism.

5. Blowing up a line, and a two-step MMP

This section does two things in succession, and they are given different inputs. The first is the Stein factorization and the smallness test, which take a morphism; the second is the MMP, which takes a ring. The join between them is marked below.

$Y$ is the blow up of $\PP^3$ along a line, and $f : Y \to \PP^3$ is the map. For the first part the package is given one thing only: a parametrization of the graph of $f$. Nothing about $f$ is stated. The graph lives in a bigraded ring, where the first block of variables is the source and the second block is the target. This is how the paper writes a morphism — and it is not what the MMP driver takes, which is why this half is not a run of the MMP.

Stein factorization. Write $f = g \circ h$, where $h$ has connected fibres and $g$ is finite. The package computes the coordinate ring of the middle variety $Z$. Two things come out of it. First, $\dim Z = 3$. Since $\dim Y = 3$ as well, the dimension does not drop, so $f$ is birational. Second, the ring of $Z$ is monograded, so the driver can go on from $Z$ without any further work.

Is the contraction small? A birational contraction is called small when its exceptional locus has codimension $2$ or more. A small contraction needs a flip. A divisorial one does not. The package finds the exceptional locus as the support of $\bigwedge^2 \Omega_{Y/Z}$.

For this $f$ the exceptional locus is the exceptional divisor $E$. The package finds dimension $2$ and codimension $1$, so the contraction is divisorial.

The same test then runs on a small contraction, for comparison. Take the ordinary double point $\{g_0g_1 = g_2g_3\}$ and blow up the ideal $(g_0,g_2)$. This is the standard small resolution. Its exceptional locus is a curve, of dimension $1$ and codimension $2$, so that contraction is small. Example 6 computes a flip for a case like this one.

The MMP. Here the input changes. The relative canonical model over $Z$ is the identity, so no flip is needed and the step is divisorial; that step is recorded, and the driver is then started on the coordinate ring of $Z$ with the step handed to it as the history of the MMP so far. So the contraction above is supplied rather than found — it was assembled from the graph, not returned by the contraction stage — and what the driver computes is what comes after it, which repeats Example 1: $\lambda = 4$, and the contraction to a point. The MMP on record is

$$\mathrm{Bl}_L\PP^3 \;\xrightarrow{\ \text{divisorial}\ }\; \PP^3 \;\xrightarrow{\ \text{fibration}\ }\; \text{point},$$

two steps, ending at a $K$-negative fibration: the first supplied from the first half of this section, the second found by the driver. Every ring in the chain is monograded, and the only bigraded ring is the graph of $f$. Examples 7 to 9 are the other way round — there the driver is given a ring and finds every step, contractions included.

6. A toric flip

Take four rays in $N = \ZZ^3$:

$$v_1 = e_1,\quad v_2 = e_2,\quad v_3 = e_3,\quad v_4 = (1,1,-2).$$

They satisfy one linear relation,

$$v_1 + v_2 = 2v_3 + v_4 ,$$

and no three of them satisfy any: each of the four $3 \times 3$ determinants is nonzero. A set that is linearly dependent but has no dependent proper subset is called a circuit, and its relation, unique up to scale, splits it into the two sides of the equation — here $\{v_1,v_2\}$ and $\{v_3,v_4\}$.

That is the configuration whose cone has exactly two triangulations, one leaving out a ray of each side. Here they are $\langle v_1v_2v_3\rangle, \langle v_1v_2v_4\rangle$, meeting along the wall $\langle v_1,v_2\rangle$, and $\langle v_1v_3v_4\rangle, \langle v_2v_3v_4\rangle$, meeting along $\langle v_3,v_4\rangle$. The two toric threefolds are small modifications of each other over the untriangulated cone $\sigma$, and $\sigma$ is the target of the flipping contraction.

The two sides of the relation have coefficient sums $1 + 1 = 2$ and $2 + 1 = 3$, and that one inequality carries both of the facts that make this a flip. It says $\sigma$ is not $\QQ$-Gorenstein: an $m$ with $\langle m, v_i \rangle = 1$ for all four rays would turn the relation into $2 = 3$. And it says the move is a flip and not a flop: on the wall curve $K \cdot C$ is the difference of the two sums, with the sign depending on which side the wall is, so $K \cdot C = -1$ on the first triangulation and $+1$ on the second.

The example builds this variety in Macaulay2. The dual cone has a Hilbert basis of five elements with three relations, and one more variable $w$ makes a projective threefold $W$. The flip is then computed as the relative canonical model

$$W^{+} = \Proj \bigoplus_{m \ge 0} H^0\bigl(W, \OO_W(mK_W)\bigr).$$

The session checks that this is a real flip: the result is not the identity and not an isomorphism, but it has the same dimension as $W$. The package also writes down the inverse rational map $W \dashrightarrow W^{+}$, checks that its equations hold, and finds its base locus $V(y_4,y_5)$. Recorded as an MMP step, this is a flipping step.

7. Four MMPs over an affine base

This session runs the MMP four times, on four unrelated threefolds, one per subsection below. Each is projective over an affine base rather than over a point: giving an ambient variable degree zero puts its coordinate in $R_0$, so $X = \Proj R$ is projective over the affine $\Spec R_0$ — where the three-fold flips are, a flipping contraction contracting a curve to a point of an affine variety. What the package is handed each time is the homogeneous coordinate ring of the variety, graded that way, and nothing else; Polyhedra appears in the session only to write the toric rings down.

7.1 A flip the package finds by itself

$Y$ is one of the two triangulations of Example 6's circuit $v_1 + v_2 = 2v_3 + v_4$, the cones $\langle v_1v_2v_3\rangle$ and $\langle v_1v_2v_4\rangle$. The second has multiplicity two, so $Y$ carries one $\tfrac12(1,1,1)$ point and its canonical index is $2$. Its wall curve has $(K \cdot C, H \cdot C) = (-1,1)$, so the threshold is $\tfrac12$, and there the wall curve — a curve, not a divisor, so the contraction is small — goes to the singular point of $\Spec R_0$. The step is a flip:

$$Y \;\dashrightarrow\; Z, \qquad Y \;\longrightarrow\; \Spec R_0 \;\longleftarrow\; Z .$$

$Z$ is the other triangulation, $\langle v_1v_3v_4\rangle$ and $\langle v_2v_3v_4\rangle$: smooth, with $K$ nef, so the MMP stops. Example 6 is handed this contraction; here it is found from $Y$ alone, and the session checks that the variety returned really is $Z$ by a degree-preserving relabelling of the variables. $Y$ is presented by the second Veronese of its section ring, because the ring itself needs a generator of degree two and the Cartier test over-reports on a weighted grading, returning the index as $1$ where it is $2$.

7.2 A divisorial step

$\mathrm{Bl}_0(\AA^3)$, as $\Proj$ of the Rees algebra of $(a,b,c)$. Here $K = 2E$ and $\OO(1) = -E$, so $K + tH = (2-t)E$ and the threshold is $2$; at the threshold the exceptional surface $E$ contracts to the origin.

$$\mathrm{Bl}_0(\AA^3) \;\longrightarrow\; \AA^3 .$$

A surface and not a curve, so the step is divisorial rather than a flip, and $K$ is nef on $\AA^3$.

7.3 A flip of canonical index three

The same construction on four rays with the circuit $v_1 + v_2 = v_3 + 3v_4$. Of its two triangulations, $Y'$ is the one on which $K$ is negative; its canonical index is $3$ and its threshold $\tfrac23$, and again the wall curve goes to the singular point of the base.

$$Y' \;\dashrightarrow\; Z' .$$

$Z'$ has canonical index $1$ and $K$ nef.

7.4 Two steps: divisorial, then a fibration

$\mathrm{Bl}_0(\AA^2) \times \PP^1$ over the affine base $\AA^2$, polarized by $\OO(1,3)$. Its two extremal curves are $E \times \mathrm{pt}$, with $(K \cdot C, H \cdot C) = (-1,1)$, and the fibre $\mathrm{pt} \times \PP^1$, with $(-2,3)$, so the thresholds are $1$ and $\tfrac23$ and the larger belongs to $E$ alone.

$$\mathrm{Bl}_0(\AA^2) \times \PP^1 \;\longrightarrow\; \AA^2 \times \PP^1 \;\longrightarrow\; \AA^2 .$$

The first arrow contracts the surface $E \times \PP^1$ to the curve $\{0\} \times \PP^1$: divisorial. The second contracts the fibres of $\AA^2 \times \PP^1$, a $K$-negative fibration, and the MMP stops. The middle variety is neither the base nor a point, so it is built as $\Proj$ over $\Spec R_0$ of the algebra the sections generate, and its Stein factorization is certified trivial rather than computed.

8. An MMP three steps long

The threefold is $X = S_3 \times \AA^1$, projective over the affine base $\AA^3 = \AA^2 \times \AA^1$, where $S_3$ is $\AA^2$ blown up three times, each time at a point of the last exceptional curve. In $N = \ZZ^2$ that is the fan of $\AA^2 = \mathrm{cone}(u_1,u_2)$, with $u_1 = (1,0)$ and $u_2 = (0,1)$, subdivided by

$$u_3 = (1,1), \qquad u_4 = u_1 + u_3 = (2,1), \qquad u_5 = u_1 + u_4 = (3,1).$$

Write $E_1, E_2, E_3$ for the divisors of $u_3, u_4, u_5$. The neighbours in the fan give $E_1^2 = E_2^2 = -2$ and $E_3^2 = -1$, and adjunction turns that into $K \cdot E_1 = K \cdot E_2 = 0$ and $K \cdot E_3 = -1$. Only $E_3$ is $K$-negative, and the same is true again after each contraction, so the MMP is forced. Polarizing by the $A$ with $A \cdot E_1 = A \cdot E_2 = A \cdot E_3 = 1$ makes every threshold $1$, and the MMP is

$$X = S_3 \times \AA^1 \;\longrightarrow\; S_2 \times \AA^1 \;\longrightarrow\; \mathrm{Bl}_0(\AA^2) \times \AA^1 \;\longrightarrow\; \AA^3 .$$

Every arrow is a divisorial contraction over $\AA^3$: the surface $E_3 \times \AA^1$ contracts to a line, then $E_2 \times \AA^1$ to a line, then $E_1 \times \AA^1$ to the axis $\{0\} \times \AA^1$. $K$ is nef on $\AA^3$, so the MMP stops there, three steps in. Both middle models are checked against ground truth built from their own polarizations, by a degree-preserving relabelling, and $\AA^3$ is handed back as $\Proj$ of $\AA^3[t]$ so that the final nefness test has a $\Proj$ to read.

The fan is only how the coordinate ring gets written down — a subring of $k[a,b,c,t]$ — and everything after that is computed from the ring. The factor $\AA^1$ rather than $\PP^1$ keeps the structure morphism birational, which is what makes each threshold one intersection number on a fibre curve.

9. Three steps ending in a flip

The base is Example 7's first row: $\Spec R_0$ is not $\QQ$-Gorenstein, and the flipping side $Y$ of the circuit has one $\tfrac12(1,1,1)$ point, the cone $\langle v_1,v_2,v_4\rangle$ of multiplicity two. The threefold here sits two divisors above $Y$, from

$$w = (1,1,-1) \quad\text{interior to that cone},\qquad w_2 = v_2 + v_4 = (1,2,-2) \quad\text{on a facet of } \sigma.$$

The star subdivision at $w$ resolves the $\tfrac12(1,1,1)$ point, giving a smooth $X_1$, and $w_2$ adds one divisor over the facet, giving $X$: smooth, projective over $\Spec R_0$, of relative Picard rank three, polarized by $H = 4D_{v_4} + D_w + 3D_{w_2}$. Its wall curves have $(K \cdot C, H \cdot C) = (-1,1)$ on $\langle w,w_2\rangle$, $(-1,2)$ on $\langle v_1,w\rangle$ and $(0,1)$ on the rest, so the threshold is $1$ and one wall alone attains it. The MMP is

$$X \;\longrightarrow\; X_1 \;\longrightarrow\; Y \;\dashrightarrow\; Z , \qquad\text{with}\qquad Y \;\longrightarrow\; \Spec R_0 \;\longleftarrow\; Z .$$

The first two arrows are divisorial contractions at $t = 1$: the surface $D_{w_2}$ contracts to a curve, then $D_w$ to the $\tfrac12(1,1,1)$ point. The third is a flip at $t = \tfrac12$: the wall curve of the circuit — a curve and not a divisor, so the contraction is small — goes to the singular point of $\Spec R_0$, and $Z$ is the other triangulation, where $K$ is nef.

What lets a flip come third is the circuit wall $\langle v_1,v_2\rangle$: it is $K$-trivial on $X$ and on $X_1$, and turns $K$-negative only on $Y$, so it cannot be contracted until the two divisors are gone. The fibre curves record that — $(K \cdot C, H \cdot C)$ runs $\{(-1,1),(0,1),(0,1)\}$, then $\{(-1,1),(0,1)\}$, then $\{(-1,1)\}$, and $\{(+1,1)\}$ on $Z$, the sign change that makes it a flip and not a flop — and the flip is the only step whose relative canonical model is not the identity.

The four coordinate rings are subrings of $k[a,b,c,t]$ over $R_0 = k[b,\, b^2c,\, a,\, abc,\, a^2c]$, given as lists of monomials rather than as fan data. All three models the MMP produces are checked against ground truth by a degree-preserving relabelling, and the last two are Example 7's $Y$ and $Z$.

Every push re-runs them and rebuilds this page, so a session that stops working stops the page from being published.

How to repeat these runs

You need Macaulay2 1.26.06 and a checkout with its submodules.

git clone --recurse-submodules https://github.com/takehikoyasuda/Coding_MMP
cd Coding_MMP
examples/run.sh                                   # all nine
examples/run.sh examples/01-projective-space.m2   # or one of them

Each run rewrites the .out transcript and the .time file next to its script. This page is built from those files, so it cannot quote output that Macaulay2 no longer prints.

What is not here

These nine examples finish. Some other inputs do not. Those cases were measured rather than guessed, and the measurements are in research-log/. What the implementation is tested and trusted to do is listed in docs/IMPLEMENTATION-STATUS.md.

The package's multigraded entry points are also not shown here. They take a multigraded variety as input, which the paper does not, so their examples belong with the package rather than with the paper.
